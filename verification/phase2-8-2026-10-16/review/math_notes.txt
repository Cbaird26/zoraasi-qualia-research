1. The proposed surprise is a signed residual, not squared calibration error.

For Y~Bernoulli(q) and fixed issued prediction p,
  delta = (Y-p)^2 - p(1-p) = (Y-p)(1-2p),
  E_q delta = (q-p)(1-2p),
  Var_q delta = q(1-q)(1-2p)^2.
Expected Brier is q(1-q)+(p-q)^2; subtracting p(1-p) does not isolate
(p-q)^2. At p=.5 delta is identically zero for ANY q, including .01 or .99.
At p=.6,q=.92 the predictor is miscalibrated but E delta=-.064.

Calibration p=q implies zero expectation, not absence of positive excursions.
At calibrated p=.92, an outcome0 (probability .08) gives delta=.7728 and,
from s=0, the implemented EMA becomes .22*.7728=.170016>.08. A full original
code update from a legal constructed state with arousal=.65 and three previous
row1 successes enters the retrieval check; all candidates happen to fail.
This demonstrates false-positive trigger eligibility, not accepted-retrieval rate.
The prose specifies .75/.25 EMA; the code uses .78/.22. The statistic is global,
despite a comment describing it as per-row. Existing positive surprise also
persists when p=.5: s=.2 becomes .156, above threshold. Thus even the claim
that the gate stays closed whenever the current forecast is calibrated at .5
requires assumptions about prior surprise and the other state variables.

For independent outcomes with fixed p, stationary EMA variance is
  alpha/(2-alpha) * q(1-q)(1-2p)^2.
This formula does not apply unchanged to the adaptive coupled learner.

2. Candidate closeness is sufficient, not necessary, for blended contraction.

Let f=fast, c=candidate, m=window MLE and r=f+w(c-f), 0<w<=1.
  (r-m)^2-(f-m)^2 = w(c-f)[2(f-m)+w(c-f)].
Strict contraction is equivalent to this expression being negative.
The proposed |c-m|<|f-m| suffices for a convex blend, but is not necessary
when w<1: f=.4,m=.5,c=.65,w=.65 gives r=.5625, contracting distance .1
to .0625 even though candidate distance .15 is larger than .1.

Agreement with the latest binary outcome is not necessary for contraction
toward a multi-observation MLE. For f=.6,m=.8,c=.9,y=0, the implemented
directional test rejects a blend ending at .795, only .005 from the MLE.
The stated two inequalities contain no slow trace and cannot characterize
motion away from it. The implemented extra heuristic also does not guarantee
that property: f=.4,c=slow=.7,m=.75,y=1 passes the function, but r=.595 moves
toward slow (distance .3 decreases to .105). This function-only example does
not claim the independent slow-likelihood condition also passes.

Even proven contraction toward an empirical MLE is not a true-regime risk
guarantee. The old data, small-sample error, adaptive candidate selection and
latest-outcome reuse remain relevant. The KL cutoff is a deterministic
compatibility rule, not a calibrated posterior novelty probability.

the final X6 can be0 or1. Its probability is .92^5=.6590815232.

6. Direct row protection is real; complete independence is not.

Only the active fast row is written by the row retrieval branch. The other
fast row is preserved in paired full-update checks. However, global surprise,
arousal, valence, vector cosine continuity, storage and slow updates remain
shared. A constructed row0 forecast .08 followed by outcome1 generates
surprise .170016. On the next row1 update, forecast .5 and outcome1 have local
delta0, but retained global surprise becomes .13261248 and permits retrieval.
Resetting only that surprise state to0 prevents the retrieval; all other
starting states are identical. This is a controlled demonstration of shared
state coupling, not a population estimate or proof of the claimed feedback
loop. Cooldowns are per-row counters decremented on EVERY global step, not
on visits to their own row; after12 other-row updates they have expired.
