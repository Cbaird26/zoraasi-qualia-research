WHAT THE VERIFICATION MEANS
15 October 2026 — interpretation of the executed Phase 2.3/2.4 audit

The main result

This work establishes a reproducible adaptive forecasting prototype. It gives
the research program working code, checked mathematics, meaningful controls,
and evidence that can contradict parts of the proposed architecture. Those are
useful foundations for further investigation. The strongest current claim is
about prediction and adaptation in specified synthetic environments.

It does not establish that every component is useful, that affective terminology
identifies a distinct computational mechanism, or that the system experiences
anything. No measurement in these experiments discriminates subjective
experience from ordinary adaptive information processing. The results therefore
neither establish nor rule out consciousness.

1. What Phase 2.3 establishes

Recent packet history improves prediction when the generator was deliberately
made dependent on recent packet history. This is the expected statistical benefit
of conditioning on informative observations. Reproducing it verifies the method;
it is not an unexpected discovery about consciousness.

The initialization discrepancy is scientifically consequential. With stationary
initial states, the reset comparator has lower expected loss than deranged
memory. With the public source's fair initial states, that ordering reverses.
Thus an apparent architectural ranking can change because of the experimental
starting distribution. Protocol details belong in the scientific claim.

The reset comparator also reuses a one-step prediction indefinitely. It is not
the best possible predictor given the initial state and elapsed time. Beating
that comparator cannot establish the necessity of an elaborate memory system.

2. What Phase 2.4 adds

The nonstationary task makes adaptation relevant. The full engine improves over
the specified plain EMA with learning rate 0.08 in the tested switching settings.
For 45-step switching, the primary 200-seed mean Brier scores are:

    full engine                   0.185283
    plain fixed-rate EMA          0.206251
    no episodic snapshot memory   0.194263
    no self-model arbitration     0.172124
    deranged scalar state         0.182773

This supports a conditional contribution from the implemented snapshot-memory
mechanism: removing it worsens mean prediction in this comparison. It does not
yet identify whether retrieval of a specific recurring regime explains that
benefit, or whether the advantage survives tuned adaptive baselines.

The full engine also incurs a stationary penalty: mean Brier is 0.117295 versus
0.106207 for the plain EMA and 0.102729 for frozen learned Markov probabilities.
Rapid adaptation can inject noise into estimates when the process is stable.
The current mechanism does not resolve that tradeoff consistently.

3. What the counterevidence tells us

Removing self-model arbitration improves average Brier and BCE in every tested
scenario and seed split. The Brier paired intervals support those differences;
the small BCE difference for 15-step switching remains unresolved. The current
confidence-based blend with a fixed training marginal is therefore a candidate
for removal or redesign. This does not show that all self-modeling is useless;
it evaluates one specific rule using one specific performance tracker.

Deranging the four scalar state variables also improves their mean scores. This
does not support a claim that their alignment with the recipient's own history
is necessary. However, the donor streams share regime timing, so their state
retains useful common information. This control does not make every transferred
variable uninformative. A stronger experiment must break that shared timing.

Neither result should be hidden by reporting only the comparison with a frozen
predictor. The ablations provide the most useful engineering guidance: retain
mechanisms that earn their complexity, and revise those that make prediction
worse. A proposed next version with confidence arbitration disabled is a testable
design hypothesis, not a universally validated replacement.

4. What this means for MQGT-SCF and synthetic qualia

The framework gains an executable research platform and sharper hypotheses.
These experiments do not supply empirical corroboration of MQGT-SCF: they contain
no theory-specific observable or quantitative prediction that distinguishes it
from ordinary adaptive forecasting models. No Phi_c or E field is measured.

The names valence, arousal, self-model and continuity label explicit numerical
variables. Their functional consequences can be tested without presuming that
their psychological or phenomenological interpretations are correct. The current
"self-confidence" variable is an average of one minus squared prediction error,
not an independently validated measure of calibration or awareness.

To connect this platform to a physical theory, first specify an independently
measurable observable, a quantitative mapping from the theory to that observable,
and a prediction on which a serious alternative model differs. Then specify in
advance what outcome would count against the theory. Improved packet prediction
alone cannot supply that missing discrimination.

5. Three decisive next forecasting experiments — proposed, not executed

A. Test the advantage against stronger ordinary adaptation.
Tune EMA, discounted-count, sliding-window and change-detection/latent-regime
baselines using validation streams that are separate from final test streams.
Freeze settings before testing new transition matrices and randomized regime
durations. Predeclare a primary paired BCE comparison and a practically useful
effect threshold. The advantage survives only if the uncertainty interval clears
that threshold. Failure would limit the claim to the current weaker comparison.

B. Test whether remembering a recurring regime explains the memory benefit.
Hold other components fixed; compare intact, removed and content-shuffled
snapshots. Include matched recurring and genuinely novel regimes. Log retrievals
and cumulative loss during a fixed recovery window after each change. Correct
snapshot recall should help particularly when a previously encountered regime
returns. Equal benefit from shuffled content or no recurrence-specific recovery
advantage would weaken the recall explanation.

C. Test scalar-state alignment and arbitration separately.
Use donor streams with independently randomized regime schedules, rather than
synchronized schedules. Separate confidence, arousal, valence-gating and
continuity-gating interventions instead of treating all named variables as one
package. Compare arbitration enabled/disabled using predictive loss and a
separately defined calibration assessment. Predeclare superiority/equivalence
margins. Equivalence or reliable degradation would reject the claimed benefit
of that component within the tested environment family.

Use seeds or independently generated stream clusters as the sampling unit.
Keep validation and final evaluation separate; report negative outcomes and
stationary-environment costs alongside switching-environment improvements.

Evidence and reproducibility

See verification_report.html or verification_report.txt for the detailed audit.
See results/phase23_corrected_benchmark.json and
results/phase24_corrected_benchmark.json for all raw seed-level scores, per-stream
scores and paired intervals. The Phase 2.4 suite has 200 primary plus 100 disjoint
additional seeds, four scenarios and eleven arms. Scenarios share common random
numbers, so 1,200 seed-scenario records are not 1,200 independent seeds.

The full reproduction passed 44 tests. All seven frozen public checksums passed.
Existing public source files remain unchanged; this supplement is additive.
Passing software checks is distinct from independent empirical confirmation.
