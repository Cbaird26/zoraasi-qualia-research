Meaning of the reference
------------------------
These are exact expected losses with known transition parameters, at a FIXED
29-transition horizon. They are also the fitted estimator's limit as the number
of independent training trials tends to infinity at that horizon. They are not
the exact expected scores after only 24 training trials, and not a prediction of
the one-seed realization. The reset and deranged controls deliberately misuse a
one-step transition forecast, so a "known-parameter reference" for these controls
does not mean a Bayes-optimal predictor for their available information. Finite
training noise and Laplace shrinkage can affect their risk in either direction.

Stationary initialization used by Gemini's reproduction
-------------------------------------------------------
If P(X0=1)=pi, every Xt has marginal pi. With t starting at 1:

  stateful Brier = v(1-lambda^2)
  stateful BCE   = (1-pi)h(a)+pi h(b)
  memoryless Brier = v; BCE = h(pi)
  deranged Brier = v(1+lambda^2)
  deranged BCE   = C_D = (1-pi)C(pi,a)+pi C(pi,b)
  reset Brier(t) = v[1+lambda^2-2 lambda^(t+1)]
  reset BCE(t)   = C_D-v lambda^t D.

For g_H=(1-lambda^H)/[H(1-lambda)]=0.09578521125246335:

  mean reset Brier = v(1+lambda^2)-2v lambda^2 g_H
  mean reset BCE   = C_D-v lambda D g_H
                   = C_D-(C_D-C_stateful)g_H.

Actual public source has a different initial distribution
--------------------------------------------------------
reference/repository/zora_lab/connection_study.py initializes each sequence with
bool(rng.getrandbits(1)), giving P(X0=1)=mu0=0.5. That differs from pi=7/9 used in
the supplied reproduction. Same-time donor marginals are mu_(t-1), not pi, for
these nonstationary finite trials. Their independence from the target still
holds when the source trial is different.

For arbitrary mu0, define:

  mu_t = pi+(mu0-pi)lambda^t
  r_j(t) = P(Xt=1 | X0=j) = pi+(j-pi)lambda^t
  qbar = (1/H) sum_(t=1)^H mu_t.

The pooled target-marginal estimator converges to qbar, not pi, at this fixed
horizon. Here qbar=0.760749295777340. The per-step exact risks for either
L=B (Brier) or L=C (BCE) are:

  stateful(t) = (1-mu_(t-1))L(a,a)+mu_(t-1)L(b,b)
  memoryless(t) = L(mu_t,qbar)
  reset(t) = (1-mu0)L(r_0(t),a)+mu0 L(r_1(t),b)
  deranged(t) = (1-mu_(t-1))L(mu_t,a)+mu_(t-1)L(mu_t,b).

Useful equivalent Brier expressions, v0=mu0(1-mu0):

  deranged(t) = mu_t(1-mu_t)+lambda^2 mu_(t-1)(1-mu_(t-1))
  reset(t) = mu_t(1-mu_t)+lambda^2 v0+(mu_1-mu_t)^2
             -2 v0 lambda^(t+1).

These use Cov(Xt,X0)=v0 lambda^t. The stationary covariance v lambda^t is not
valid when mu0=0.5. Averaged memoryless Brier=qbar(1-qbar); BCE=h(qbar).

The ranking changes: reset is WORSE than deranged for both scores. Its forecasts
remain tied to a balanced initial state, so their mean stays mu_1=0.6, even as
the target's mean approaches 7/9. Deranged forecasts reflect the changing donor
marginals. Therefore Gemini's stationary reset < deranged ranking must not be
used as a deterministic invariant or a benchmark for the public generator.

If one instead forces the memoryless prediction to pi under the public
generator, its expected Brier is 0.182299773951 and BCE is 0.551038850717. That is
a distinct comparison, not the training-limit pooled marginal used above.

Interpretation
--------------
Autocorrelation was explicitly built into the generator, so improvement from
using the actual previous packet is expected. The reset control is intentionally
weak: an initial-state-aware, time-aware predictor would use r_j(t), rather than
reuse a one-step prediction indefinitely. None of these calculations tests
subjective experience, synthetic qualia, or a physical theory of consciousness.
