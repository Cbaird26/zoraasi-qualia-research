
Phase 2.6 verification: faster switching, unresolved novelty harm
=================================================================

Executed October 15, 2026. This audit preserves the supplied script, isolates its syntax repair, corrects software defects in a separate engine, and tests the proposed novelty-aware retrieval gate against actual pinned Phase 2.5 code. Lower Brier and binary cross-entropy (BCE, natural-log nats) are better. All earlier public artifacts remain unchanged.

Conclusion: the proposed package improves the first ten predictions after switches against the specified validation-tuned baselines, and retains a familiar-return advantage over its own no-memory arm. It does not eliminate harmful memory reuse on the novel blocks. The Bayesian gate itself has little measured incremental effect at these settings. Faster plasticity and prediction damping are more strongly supported components. Phase 2.6 is not uniformly better than Phase 2.5.

3. Mathematics: correct evidence, unsupported certainty
=======================================================

The implemented integral is correct for an ordered Markov path conditional on its starting state, under independent Beta(1,1) priors for p11 and p01: log evidence = log B(N11+1,N10+1) + log B(N01+1,N00+1). Tests verify exact factorial values, equality with sequential Beta predictive probabilities, and normalization over feasible paths. It is not the raw Beta-binomial count probability mass. Do not add combinatorial factors only to the novelty denominator. Valid count-event multiplicities must be included consistently and cancel in the corresponding likelihood ratio; whole Markov paths do not generally have multiplicity equal to the product of row binomial coefficients.

The proposed six-transition example (N11,N10,N01,N00)=(4,0,2,0) is impossible for one contiguous binary path: N01−N10 equals the end state minus the start state, and therefore must be −1, 0, or 1. A realizable replacement is 0,1,0,1,1,1,1, giving counts (3,1,2,0). It still illustrates a snapshot that beats a stale point estimate but loses to the integrated prior. This is a likelihood illustration, not a replay proving the old full engine would retrieve: fast adaptation and the arousal/continuity eligibility conditions also matter.

The stated retrieval penalty also treats blending as complete replacement. Phase 2.5 uses 65% snapshot plus 35% current estimate. With current p01=.85, snapshot .28, and true probability .88, the updated estimate is .4795; expected Brier increases by .15950025, not .36. That is conditional on visiting previous state 0, before subsequent adaptation. Likewise, (.92−.15)^2=.5929 is squared bias, while expected Brier is .7204 and realized losses are .8464 or .0064. A forecast exactly equal to .5 has Brier .25, but the supplied CUSUM emits its forecast before resetting and immediately updates afterward; there is no system-wide .25 cap.

The earlier Phase 2.5 validation-selected EMA rate was .32, not the proposed .14–.18 range. The claimed arousal plateau near .16 was not measured in the proposal. Its rolling buffer is not cleared on surprise and therefore is not guaranteed to contain only post-switch observations.

Passing this gate does not verify familiarity. A feasible six-transition run of ones passes snapshot A’s gate, yet has probability .88^6 = .464404086784 under novel D conditional on initial state 1. Unobserved or weakly observed transition rows remain ambiguous. The code checks rows only when their count is at least 2, whereas the prose says every observed row; it accepts equality at the row floor and uses retrieval cooldown 6, not the prose’s 8. A singleton-row counterexample passes the joint gate despite violating the stated per-row floor.

The prior, independent-row assumption, constant-regime assumption within the window, thresholds and window length are modeling choices. Fast parameters are adapted using data from the scored window, stored snapshots may overlap it, and the best snapshot is selected among candidates. The resulting adaptive selection rule is not a calibrated posterior probability of novelty or familiarity. A Bayes-style score can be a useful predictive heuristic without supplying that stronger interpretation.


4. Corrections and controlled comparisons
=========================================

The corrected engine preserves the proposed numerical defaults, including the code’s row minimum 2 and cooldown 6. It repairs the repeated valence-ordering bug by scoring the online marginal before updating it with the current outcome. It also validates inputs, resets all state, uses learned initialization, and honors disabled or zero-capacity memory. A diagnostic legacy_marginal_order flag isolates the original biased comparison. Predictions remain causal: current outcomes change subsequent forecasts, not the current scored forecast.

Nine Phase 2.6 arms separate full behavior, gate-off, fallback-and-damping-off, both gate/fallback-off, no-memory, no-memory/no-fallback, surprise-boost-off, prediction-damping-off, and legacy marginal ordering. Additional arms include actual Phase 2.5 with and without memory, newly tuned EMA/CUSUM, the earlier fixed .32 EMA and .20/1.0/.38 CUSUM, a frozen learned model, and a privileged true-regime oracle. The fallback-off switch disables both the local reset and forecast damping; damping-off independently removes only damping. No pure reset-only intervention is claimed.

All deployable arms receive the same learned estimates from 24 independent stationary-A trials of 30 states each, using Beta(1,1) smoothing, and the same target observations. Training and observation RNG domains are distinct. The supplied nine-block schedule is A/B/A/B/C/D/E/B/A, with E=(.08,.12). The six prior scenarios are unchanged. Novel masks cover C/D/E first-visit blocks; familiar-return masks cover repeated blocks. An initially trained A and a first test visit to B are not equivalent forms of familiarity.

Baseline selection uses only mean validation Brier on the nine-block mixed schedule: 50 EMA candidates and 384 CUSUM candidates. This includes every supplied grid point and the earlier selected CUSUM setting. Selected EMA rate is 0.27; selected CUSUM rate=0.18, drift=0.4, threshold=1.0. The original unclipped EMA/CUSUM families are retained with learned initial estimates. The saved manifest includes all candidate results and is written before evaluation. These choices are applied unchanged to all seven scenarios. Engine thresholds were not tuned after these results.

Gate-off scores are identical on 97/100 primary and 94/100 follow-up runs. The gate blocks every otherwise advantageous candidate on only 3 primary and 7 follow-up steps in total across those 100-run cohorts. Individual candidate rejections are a different counter and can occur while another candidate is accepted. The measured full-versus-gate-off difference intervals include zero across the displayed scopes. Thus the data do not establish useful incremental novelty protection from this gate at these settings.

The instantaneous squared-error boost has a much larger early-window effect: removing it raises Brier by about .034 on the mixed task in both cohorts. Removing prediction damping also worsens early and novel-block scores, though by a smaller amount. Local OOD resets occur only .13 and .12 times per 405-transition run on average. These controlled ablations support the contribution of update responsiveness and damping in this configuration; they do not prove a unique mechanism or assign additive percentages to interacting components.

Retrieval still occurs during first-visit novel blocks. Such an event is not automatically a false identification: a snapshot may have been stored earlier within that same block. The recorded block-level events permit analysis without equating a code counter named familiar_retrievals with ground-truth recognition.

On recurring A/B regimes, Phase 2.6 is worse overall by Brier than actual Phase 2.5 despite better early-switch performance. It is also worse on stationary A, where the frozen learned model remains strongest among these three. It improves the tested unseen-random scenario. This pattern supports a responsiveness-versus-stability tradeoff, not a general upgrade. Validation baselines were selected on one mixed task and are not individually optimized for every scenario.

Intervals are pointwise normal Monte Carlo intervals across independent seed replicates, calculated on paired differences. They do not account for multiplicity, prior architectural exploration, or model-selection uncertainty. No equivalence/noninferiority margin was prespecified, so an interval spanning zero does not establish matching performance. Follow-up seeds are fresh but use the same generators, not external replication or preregistration. Fixed novel matrices, fixed block order, one training regime, and endogenously accumulated snapshots limit generality.

The proposal’s donor discussion is directionally useful but remains too strong about exact timing pulses. Shared schedules can confound interpretation without leaking the current target into a prior forecast; donor errors and scalar responses are stochastic. Independent donor schedules can change regime exposure as well as timing. No new donor-transfer experiment was implemented in this focused Phase 2.6 audit, and no isolation claim beyond the prior measured controls is made.


9. What it means and the next justified experiment
==================================================

The work now separates two research claims that the supplied proposal bundled together: faster response is supported on the tested switching task, while novelty-safe retrieval is not. The next experiment should target the latter directly. Develop and freeze a rule on a separate development bank, then test whether it reduces novel-block excess loss without losing familiar-return gains. Include no-memory and gate-off controls, log support for each transition row, and distinguish old-regime snapshots from snapshots formed inside a newly encountered block.

The gate should be evaluated as a calibrated decision rule under explicit prior and observation assumptions, with counterbalanced regime orders, more transition matrices and variable dwell times. A useful test should show that the gate actually changes enough retrieval decisions to explain improved prediction. These are proposed next steps, not claimed results or silent modifications to this audited design.

Arousal, valence, continuity and novelty are names for computable internal variables and decisions here. Predictive improvements, Beta evidence, and retrieval counters do not establish phenomenological experience or empirical support for MQGT-SCF. The concrete achievement is a reproducible statistical learning experiment whose failure modes remain visible.
